## What "hidden clearance" actually is (and how it differs from Rollbacks)

Hidden clearance is a regular clearance item that got marked down but was never relocated, never advertised, and often never updated on Walmart.com. The price drops, the yellow sticker goes on, and the item stays right where it always was. You only find it by scanning or knowing the codes.

It helps to separate Walmart's five discount mechanisms, because people routinely confuse them and then wonder why the "clearance" tag only saved them 15%:

| Deal type | Typical discount | How long it lasts | Where you find it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rollback | 10–30% | 30–90 days | Original aisle, no relocation |
| Regular clearance | 30–60% | Until sold out | Original aisle or clearance endcap |
| Hidden clearance | 50–90% | Until scanned out | Original aisle, never relocated |
| Flash deal | 15–50% | 2–7 days | Online/app only, timed |
| Salvage / final markdown | 80–95% | Days, not weeks | Scattered aisles + back-of-store carts |

The distinction matters practically. A Rollback is Walmart promoting a still-stocked item — the price is decent but there's more supply coming. Clearance means the system has given up on the SKU. Hidden clearance is simply clearance that never got featured, which is exactly why the discounts run so deep: nobody's buying what nobody can see.

According to aggregated reports from Walmart employees on the r/walmart community, roughly 40–60% of a Supercenter's clearance inventory at any given moment is "hidden" in this way. Walk straight to the orange clearance signage and you're skipping more than half the marked-down stock in the building.

## The yellow sticker code ladder, decoded

The two digits after the dollar sign on a yellow clearance sticker tell you which markdown stage the item is in. This is the single most useful piece of knowledge for in-store clearance hunting, and it comes from long-running employee reports cross-checked against 2026 price scans by deal-tracking sites.

| Sticker ends in | Stage | Average discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $.06 | First markdown | ~18% off |
| $.04 | First markdown (deeper) | ~32% off |
| $.02 | Second markdown | ~48% off |
| $.00 | Stable clearance | ~65% off |
| $.03 | Deep clearance | ~78% off |
| $.07 | Final markdown / salvage | ~92% off (often 80–95%) |

A few things worth knowing about how this plays out in practice:

- **$.04 items can bounce back.** First-round markdowns sometimes get reversed to the original price, so if you want it and it's at $.04, don't assume it'll still be there (or still discounted) next week.
- **$.07 is the bottom.** Once an item hits salvage tier, employees report that the register price frequently comes out $1–3 *below* even the sticker price, because the system overrides the printed label. The standard advice is to scan any $.07 sticker in the Walmart app immediately before assuming the sticker is the final number.
- The ladder isn't a guarantee — it's an average across thousands of scanned items. Individual stores and managers vary. But as a triage tool, it's remarkably consistent: if you've got ten minutes and a full aisle, scan the $.00–$.07 stickers first and ignore the $.06s.

## When Walmart actually marks things down

Markdowns follow a weekly and monthly rhythm, and timing your trip matters as much as knowing the codes.

Deal-tracking sites that aggregate markdown scans put the odds of finding fresh yellow stickers like this: **Monday 84%**, Wednesday 76%, Tuesday 48%, Friday 31%, Sunday 22%, Saturday 14%. The reason is logistics, not marketing. Most Supercenters receive shipments Sunday nights and Tuesday nights, and department managers walk the aisles the next morning clearing out items past their hold date — which is when停产 items get pushed to the next markdown tier. By the weekend, the good stuff has already been picked over by Monday-to-Wednesday shoppers.

The same tracking data points to two more windows:

- **Wednesday before 10am** is the classic clearance run. Managers scan items overnight Tuesday into Wednesday and apply the next tier; early shoppers see the new prices before the crowds.
- **The last three days of the month** get a deeper sweep, because inventory managers want clean numbers before reporting cycles. Items already marked down twice often take a third cut here.

Seasonally, the pattern holds too: winter apparel clears in early March and summer apparel in late August, outdoor and garden items mark down from late August through October and again in January, and holiday-specific aisles typically start at 50–75% off the morning after the holiday.

One rumor to kill: the internet loves the idea of "penny items" — clearance stock dropping to $0.01. This used to happen occasionally, but Walmart has largely eliminated penny pricing in most regions. Chase the 50–70% cuts instead; they're real and reliable.

Also worth noting: in-store markdowns usually hit before they show up on Walmart.com. The site's clearance and Flash Deals pages do update multiple times a day, so they're worth checking if you're shopping online — just know the shelf in front of you may be cheaper than what the app shows, and vice versa.
